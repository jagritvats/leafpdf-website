Why use an offline PDF editor

Updated 19 November 2026 · Leaf 0.2.2

PDFs are often the documents that matter most: contracts, tax returns, medical letters, pay slips, IDs. Where you edit them decides who else handles them.

What happens when you edit a PDF online

A web-based editor works on a server. To change your PDF, it has to:

  1. upload the whole file, including any hidden parts such as metadata, attachments and earlier revisions;
  2. store and process it on a computer run by someone else, in a country you may not know;
  3. send the result back, and keep or delete the file according to that service's policy.

Many services handle this carefully. Still, the document has left your hands, and you depend on someone else's security, retention rules and staff. For some documents that alone is a problem: contracts, health records and client files often come with confidentiality duties or data-protection rules about who may receive them.

What changes with a local editor

  • The file stays put. It is read from your disk, edited in memory and saved back. No copy is made anywhere else.
  • It works without a connection, on a train, on a locked-down network or when a service is down.
  • No upload limits. Big scans and long documents open as fast as your computer allows.
  • You can check it. With an open-source editor, anyone can read the code and see what it does with your files.

A local editor doesn't make a file safe on its own: your computer still needs to be secure, and once you email the result, it's out. But it removes one step where a copy could leak.

How Leaf handles your files

Leaf has no accounts, analytics, telemetry, crash reporting or ads. Documents are opened, edited and saved on your computer and are never uploaded anywhere.

It goes online only when you ask for something that needs it:

  • Extra OCR languages: Add languages… downloads the language files you pick. No document content is sent.
  • Timestamped signatures: if you enter a timestamp server, it receives a hash of the signature, never the document.
  • Links in documents: a web link you click opens in your browser.

Recovery copies of unsaved work are kept in your user profile and deleted when you save or close the document. The full details are in the privacy policy, and the code is on GitHub under the AGPL.

Before you share a PDF

Keeping a file local is half of it. Before it leaves your computer, check what it carries:

  • Black boxes drawn over text don't remove it. Redact it for real.
  • Metadata, comments, attachments and hidden text can hold more than the visible page. Leaf's Sanitize document removes them.
  • A picture of a signature proves little. For proof of who signed and that nothing changed, use a digital signature.
